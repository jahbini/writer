Scene:  
The sun’s just hit the top of the hill behind the cottage, painting the gorse in burnt gold. I’m sitting on the porch step, sipping tea that’s gone slightly sour—probably just left it too long in the tin. The air smells like wet stone and old paper, the kind of smell that sticks to your coat even after you’ve wiped it down. Down the lane, a girl with a dog is walking slowly, the dog a little scruffy, wagging like it’s trying to say something in a language only tails understand. I don’t know her, but I don’t need to. Just watching her, the way the light catches the edge of her hat, makes me feel like I’m remembering something I forgot how to forget. Quiet, really. Like the world is breathing in slow rhythm.

Arrival:  
I’d just stepped off the bus, the one that runs every Thursday and always smells like damp wool and someone’s forgotten lunch. My boots were already cold from the walk, and the wind had a way of biting at your ankles like it’s trying to remind you you’re not supposed to be here. I remember the moment I saw the cottage—two red bricks, a crooked door, a window with a pane that’s cracked in the middle, like it’s seen too many storms. My hands were shaking a little, not from cold, but from the way the silence just… settled. I thought I’d hear something—maybe a dog, maybe a kettle—anything to break the stillness. But it didn’t. Just me, the wind, and the smell of woodsmoke that wasn’t quite there, like it was waiting to be noticed.

Disturbance:  
Then, out of nowhere, a noise—like a door slamming, but not in the cottage. I turned. No one. Just the wind again, but louder, like it’d been caught off guard. Then, a sound from the kitchen—someone stirring, or maybe a pan. I went inside, just to check. The kitchen was empty. The stove was off. A teacup sat on the counter, cold, with a single drop of milk that hadn’t been stirred. I stood there, heart doing a little flip, like it had forgotten how to beat slowly. I thought maybe it was the dog, or maybe the wind had gone mad. But when I looked again, the door was still closed, and the light was on. Just a flicker. Like something had been watching me, and then decided to disappear.

Reflection:  
I sat back on the step, the tea now cold and forgotten. I didn’t feel angry, not really. Just… still. Like the world had paused, and I’d been the one who’d forgotten how to move. I thought about the way the dog had looked at me earlier, like it knew something I didn’t. I thought about the tea, the cracked window, the way the light fell across the floor like it was trying to say something polite. Nothing dramatic. Just a quiet hum beneath the surface, like the house had its own rhythm, and I’d just arrived too late to hear the beginning.

Realization:  
It wasn’t the dog. It wasn’t the wind. It wasn’t the tea. It was just me. I’d been so used to rushing, to needing something to happen—something loud, something sharp—to prove I was here. But here, in this quiet, with the smell of damp earth and the silence that doesn’t need to be broken, I finally feel like I’m not just passing through. I’m just… present. Like the cottage doesn’t need me to do anything. It just needs me to sit. And maybe, just maybe, that’s enough.
